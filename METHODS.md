# Methods

## Tasks and targets

Machine learning models were used to predict reporter behavior from the characteristics of the genomic context at the site of their integration. The analysis included three datasets. The first consisted of two promoterless reporters, Regular and PCPA, for which expression was measured. The second included eight intron reporters with expression measured. The third included the same eight intron reporters with splicing efficiency measured.

For each experiment, regression and classification tasks were solved separately. Within each task, two models were trained – one on integrations located inside MANE genes and one on integrations located outside MANE genes.

For the expression models, the target was the mean number of reads assigned to each barcode, normalized to library size. In the regression task, the value log10(x+1) was predicted, where x is the normalized expression. In the classification task, barcodes were divided into two classes using a normalized expression threshold of 0.1.

For the splicing models, the target was splicing efficiency, defined as the ratio of the number of reads of the spliced form to the total number of reads of the spliced and unspliced forms. In the regression task, splicing efficiency was used without further transformation. In the classification task, barcodes were divided into three classes with boundaries at 0.288 and 0.692, corresponding to the tertiles of the splicing efficiency distribution in the pooled sample.

Changes in splicing upon SON degradation were analyzed separately. Cells were treated with a compound inducing SON degradation or with vehicle as a control, and for each barcode the number of reads of the spliced and unspliced forms was determined in both conditions. Differential analysis was performed with DESeq2, and the target was the log2FC of the spliced-to-unspliced ratio upon SON degradation relative to the control. Negative values correspond to a decrease in splicing efficiency. Only barcodes for which the standard error of the averaged log2FC did not exceed 1.5 were included in the analysis.

Because log2FC is a continuous quantity, the zero class for the classification task had to be defined explicitly. A barcode was classified as affected by SON degradation if its log2FC was negative and its unadjusted p < 0.005, and as unaffected if p >= 0.005 and the absolute value of log2FC was less than 0.1. Barcodes that met neither of these criteria were not used in training. In the regression task, the log2FC value itself was predicted for barcodes classified as affected. Models were trained for the DDX51 and NBEAL2 reporters.

## Features and the two tables, inside and outside genes

The features were characteristics of the genomic environment of the integration site. They included histone modifications, RNA polymerase II ChIP-seq signal, chromatin accessibility, ChromHMM chromatin state, characteristics of nuclear organization (distance to nuclear speckles, TSA-seq data and SPIN state), the level of transcription in the integration region and host gene expression, GC content, evolutionary conservation, overlap with repeats, chromosome, distance to the nearest CAGE cluster and the NMD feature (NMD_expected).

Features measured in several replicates were averaged before training, which reduced 51 genomic tracks to 39 features. Categorical features were one-hot encoded after feature selection.

Features were combined into 17 functional groups, for which group SHAP was then computed, with the nmd group present only in the Regular and PCPA dataset. The groups were composed as follows.
histone_marks: H2AFZ, H3K27ac, H3K27me3, H3K36me3, H3K4me1, H3K4me2, H3K4me3, H3K79me2, H3K9ac, H3K9me1, H3K9me3, H4K20me1, spKAS-seq.
pol2: ENCSR000BKR-POLR2AphosphoS5, ENCSR000BMR-POLR2A, ENCSR000DLY-POLR2A, ENCSR000EGF-POLR2AphosphoS2, ENCSR000EHF-POLR2AphosphoS2, ENCSR000EHL-POLR2A, ENCSR000FAJ-POLR2A, ENCSR031TFS-POLR2A, ENCSR388QZF-POLR2A, GSE81553_K562_Smoothed_Pol2_ChIP-Seq.
nuclear_org: GSE81553_K562_HiC_100kb_Eigenvector, LaminAC_TSA-Seq, LaminB_TSA-Seq, Pol2_TSA-Seq, SON_TSA-Seq, SPIN, Speckle_distance, pSC35_TSA-Seq.
gene_annotations: DistToTES, DistToTSS, MANE_antisense, MANElength, RelPos, annot_rna_type, gencode_noncoding.

For each experiment, models were trained on two tables. The first included integrations located inside MANE genes, and the second those located outside them. For integrations outside genes, nine features are undefined and were excluded from the table: host gene length (MANElength), distances to the transcription start and end (DistToTSS, DistToTES), relative position within the transcript (RelPos), distances to the boundaries of the nearest intron (DistToIntron5, DistToIntron3), relative position within the intron (RelIntronPos), host gene expression (MANE_all_exp.norm.log10) and the NMD feature (NMD_expected). Inside genes the model received 60 features, and outside genes 51. In the two intron reporter datasets the NMD feature was not used, so there were 59 features inside genes. Outside genes, the intron_annotations and mane_expression groups dropped out entirely, and in the Regular and PCPA dataset the nmd group as well. The gene_annotations group was reduced from seven features to three, so that outside genes there were 14 groups in all datasets.

Barcodes with an undefined ChromHMM state were excluded from both tables. Barcodes falling into ChromHMM states represented by fewer than ten barcodes were also removed, with the set of such states taken as the union over the two tables so that the sets of one-hot columns in them matched. In the table of integrations inside genes, the remaining missing values were filled with zero.

## The NMD feature

The NMD feature (NMD_expected) indicated whether the reporter integration is expected to make the host gene transcript an NMD target. It was calculated for each barcode by the 50-nucleotide rule: NMD was expected if, after reporter integration, a premature stop codon arose in the transcript more than 50 nucleotides upstream of the last exon-exon junction before the gene's own stop codon. The MANE v1.0 annotation, which includes MANE Select and MANE Plus Clinical transcripts, was used, and distances were measured along the mature mRNA sequence. An integration was assigned to a gene if it fell within the gene's transcript on the same strand. If transcripts of different genes or two transcripts of the same gene overlapped at that position, the transcript with the largest genomic length was chosen.

The calculation depended on which part of the gene the integration fell into. Integrations into the 5'UTR and 3'UTR were considered not to trigger NMD. An integration into an exon of the coding region splits that exon in two, and the sequence that will enter the mature mRNA cannot be determined from coordinates. Such integrations were therefore classified only by which exon they fell into: an integration into the exon containing the gene's own stop codon was considered not to trigger NMD, and an integration into any of the preceding exons was considered to trigger it. These rules were based on the reporter sequence: it contains stop codons in several reading frames, so an integration into a coding exon creates a premature stop codon. Upon integration into an intron of the coding region, the reporter exons are inserted into the mature mRNA between the two gene exons flanking that intron, and for such integrations the sequence of this mRNA was assembled. The reporter exon carries an 18-nucleotide barcode, unique to each integration, so the barcode of the given integration, which may itself contain a stop codon, was substituted into the sequence. In the resulting RNA sequence, the first stop codon was found by reading from the gene's start codon in its reading frame and skipping TGA codons that encode selenocysteine. If this stop codon lay downstream of the gene's own stop codon, there was no premature stop codon and NMD was not expected. In all other cases the 50-nucleotide rule was applied.

For the Regular and PCPA reporters, the mature mRNA was assumed to include a 75-nucleotide cryptic exon from the piggyBac terminal repeat and the β-globin exon with the barcode, 400 nucleotides in total. The reporter exons were also assumed to always be spliced into the gene transcript, and the polyadenylation site of the PCPA reporter was not taken into account. In the intron reporters, the intron under study lies between the reporter exons and can be either excised or retained, so one integration can give rise to two mRNA forms. For the intron reporters the calculation was performed for both forms, and both forms gave the same result for all barcodes, because the insert of these reporters contains a stop codon in all three reading frames already upstream of the intron under study. For the same reason, for the intron reporters NMD was expected for any integration into the coding region of the gene except the exon with the gene's stop codon, and the feature merely reflected the position of the integration.

The feature took the value 1 if NMD was expected and 0 otherwise. It was used only in the models for the Regular and PCPA reporters trained on integrations inside MANE genes, and was not used in the models for the intron reporters, including the SON degradation models.

## Leakage-free data splitting

Data were split by groups of spatially close integrations. The genomic interval of each integration was extended by 50 bp on each side, after which overlapping intervals within one chromosome were merged into one group. Each group as a whole was assigned to only one part of the data split before training, which prevented data leakage, that is, closely located integrations ending up in both the training and the validation or test data.

Fifteen percent of barcodes were set aside as an independent holdout test set, and the remaining 85% formed the training pool, which was split in the same way into three folds for cross-validation. The split was performed separately for each model, including separately for the models trained on integrations inside and outside MANE genes, with a fixed random seed of 2005.

## Training and hyperparameter selection

Models were trained with XGBoost version 3.0.3. Hyperparameters were selected by three-fold cross-validation on the training pool. Each of the three folds served once as the validation fold, and the other two were used for training. The maximum tree depth (max_depth) was searched over the range from 1 to 8. The number of boosting iterations for each combination was determined by early stopping, with training stopped after 500 consecutive boosting rounds without improvement on the validation fold (early_stopping_rounds = 500). Early stopping monitored the fraction of misclassified barcodes on the validation fold for classification models and RMSE for regression models. The maximum number of boosting iterations was 1000 for classification models and 3000 for regression models.

Of all combinations of max_depth value and validation fold, the one giving the best performance on its validation fold was chosen, namely the highest macro-F1 for classification models and the lowest RMSE for regression models. The final number of boosting iterations was taken from the same combination, that is, equal to the number of iterations determined by early stopping on that validation fold. Metrics were not averaged over the three folds.

With the selected max_depth and number of boosting iterations, the model was retrained on the full training pool without early stopping and then evaluated once on the holdout test set. The test set was used neither for hyperparameter selection, nor for early stopping, nor for determining the thresholds that define the classes. Features with zero variance in the training pool were excluded from training, and the same set of features was removed from the validation and test data, which prevented the use of test set information in feature selection.

The remaining hyperparameters were fixed. For classification models, objective = multi:softprob, eval_metric = [mlogloss, merror], learning_rate = 0.03, subsample = 0.6, colsample_bytree = 0.5, min_child_weight = 25, gamma = 2 and reg_lambda = 12 were used. For regression models, objective = reg:squarederror, eval_metric = rmse, learning_rate = 0.03, subsample = 0.8, colsample_bytree = 0.8, min_child_weight = 5, gamma = 0.1, reg_lambda = 1 and max_bin = 300 were used. In both tasks tree_method = hist and GPU training (device = cuda) were used. The random seed for tree construction was 5673. For tables containing fewer than 4000 barcodes, stochastic row subsampling in classification models was disabled (subsample = 1) both during hyperparameter selection and in the final training. In the classification models for the SON degradation experiment, min_child_weight = 3 was used.

## Metrics

Model performance was evaluated on the holdout test set. For classification tasks, ROC AUC was used. In two-class tasks this is the standard ROC AUC. In the classification of splicing efficiency there are three classes, and there a weighted ROC AUC was used: for each class, the ROC AUC of that class against the other two was computed, after which the three values were averaged with weights equal to the class proportions in the sample. For regression tasks, the Spearman rank correlation coefficient between predicted and observed values was used.

## Group SHAP

The contribution of features to model predictions was assessed with SHAP values computed not for individual features but for whole functional groups of features, such as all histone modifications or all characteristics of nuclear organization. Simply summing the absolute SHAP values within a group is not suitable for this, because the absolute value is taken before summation and contributions of individual features acting in opposite directions do not cancel each other out, so the estimate for a group grows with the number of columns it contains.

Instead, a group of features was treated as a single player in a cooperative game, and its Shapley value was computed by exact enumeration of all coalitions of groups, without sampling. A coalition was defined as an arbitrary subset S of feature groups, and the value of a coalition was the model prediction averaged over background barcodes. To obtain it, for each background barcode a row was constructed in which the columns of the groups in S were taken from the explained barcode and all other columns from the background barcode, and the model predictions were averaged over all such rows.

The contribution of a group for an individual explained barcode was computed as follows. For each coalition S not containing the group in question, the increment in value upon adding the group was found, that is, the difference between the value of the coalition of S together with this group and the value of the coalition of S alone.

The increments of the same group differ between coalitions, because a group adds less if part of its information is already carried by the groups in S. The Shapley value of a group is by definition its increment averaged over all orders of sequentially adding all k feature groups, starting from the empty coalition. The increment depends only on which groups were added before the group in question, not on the order in which they were added. Therefore the orders were not enumerated, and the increment for each coalition S was counted once with a weight equal to the fraction of orders in which exactly S precedes the group in question. The groups in S can come before the group in question in any order, and the remaining groups after it also in any order, so this fraction equals s!(k-s-1)!/k!, where s is the number of groups in S. In each order exactly one set S precedes the group, so the weights sum to one and the contribution of the group is a weighted mean of its increments. The contribution has a sign: it is positive if the group raises the prediction for the explained barcode relative to the mean prediction over background barcodes (see below), and negative if it lowers it. The final characteristic of a group was the mean absolute value of its contribution over all explained barcodes.

For each model, 250 barcodes were drawn at random without replacement from the training pool with random seed 2005, of which 200 served as explained and 50 as background barcodes. The set of 50 background barcodes was shared by all 200 explained barcodes, and each explained barcode was paired with each of them. All columns of the groups not in the coalition were taken from the same background barcode rather than from different background barcodes for different columns, which preserved the integrity of the one-hot encoding and the correlations between features within a group.

Coalition values were computed in margin space rather than in the space of class probabilities. The margin is the sum of the outputs of the model's trees. In regression models it coincides with the model prediction, and in classification models the softmax function converts it into class probabilities. Margin space was used because it is there that the model is additive: each tree adds its output to the margin. The softmax function, in contrast, is non-linear, and the same change in margin noticeably changes a probability close to 0.5 and hardly changes a probability close to 0 or 1. In probability space, therefore, the contribution of a group would also depend on the other groups: if they had already brought the class probability to 0.99, even the most informative group could add no more than 0.01 to it. In addition, the SHAP values of individual features were also computed in margin space, so the group values could be compared with them directly.

In classification tasks, the model outputs a separate margin value for each class. What matters for the class probabilities, however, is not the margin values themselves but the differences between them: if the same quantity is added to the margins of all classes, the probabilities after softmax do not change. The contribution of a group to the margin of an individual class is therefore defined only up to a shift common to all classes. To remove this ambiguity, the contributions were centered across classes. For each explained barcode and each group, the mean contribution of this group over all classes was computed and subtracted from its contribution to each class. After this the mean contribution over classes became zero, and only the differences between classes remained. In a two-class task, after such centering, the contributions to the two classes have the same magnitude and opposite signs. Therefore the final characteristic of a group does not depend on which class is chosen as positive. It was computed as the absolute value of the centered contribution averaged over classes and over all explained barcodes.

To compare the distribution of importance between models predicting expression and splicing efficiency, group values were converted to shares. In each model, the value of each group was divided by the sum of the values of all groups of that model, so that the shares in one model summed to one. The shares of each group were averaged over the eight intron reporters separately for expression and splicing models and separately for integrations inside and outside MANE genes. For each group, its mean share in the expression regression models was compared with its mean share in the splicing regression models. The spread of a group's share between reporters was characterized by the interquartile range over the eight reporters, and the agreement between the two sets of shares was assessed by the Spearman rank correlation coefficient.

## Software

Data preparation, model training and SHAP computation were performed in Python 3.11.13 using the packages xgboost 3.0.3, shap 0.48.0, scikit-learn 1.7.1, numpy 2.2.6, pandas 2.3.1, scipy 1.16.0, intervaltree 3.0.2, matplotlib 3.10.0 and seaborn 0.13.2.
